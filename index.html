01THE CONNECTION

More visitors. Better lives?

Our starting idea is simple: a popular destination should also be a place where people can build a working life. That is why we connect sustainable tourism with decent work.

IMAGINE ONE HOTEL WORKER

We want this worker to have opportunities in January as well as August. A longer tourism season could make that possible.

02 / TOURISM STRATEGY 2027

A calendar that works beyond summer.

Strategy 2027 promotes year-round tourism. Portugal also launched the €2.5 million Integrar para o Turismo pilot in 2024, combining technical skills, Portuguese lessons and employer matching for migrant workers.

WHAT PORTUGAL ACTUALLY DOES

Pay for hiring. Build in continuity.

  1. €6,445.56 per eligible hire

    The 2026 base grant equals 12 IAS. Employers must create a net increase in employment.

  2. At least 24 months of employment retention

    Supported contracts are permanent and full-time. The grant is paid in 40% / 40% / 20% stages; the last payment follows month 24.

  3. Three months of tutoring—or 40 hours of training

    Provide tutored workplace training for at least three months, or certified external training of at least 40 hours (or the collective-agreement duration). Pay must meet the applicable wage floor.

Our view: employers receive support together with a commitment to lasting jobs.

Portugal's advantage: lower unemployment and a more balanced visitor calendar. We think its next step is to turn this strong base into better career prospects: unemployment fell by only 0.55 percentage points between 2021 and 2025.

WHO IS BETTER POSITIONED FOR 2030?

Our choice is Portugal.

We put more weight on the lower current unemployment rate and the more balanced tourism calendar. Together, they give Portugal the stronger starting position for advancing 8.5 and 8.9.

Greece deserves credit, too.

Its unemployment gap with Portugal narrowed by 5.57 percentage points in four years. We expect further catch-up if that recovery continues and tourism spreads beyond the summer peak. Equal pay and job security remain priorities for both countries.

05THE TAKEAWAY

Our verdict. Our hopes for 2030.

We finish with a clear position and a practical ambition: fewer people out of work, and more tourism activity spread across the year.

01

Portugal: ahead, but still room to improve.

At 6.16%, unemployment is lower; the four-year decline was only 0.55 points. We would now push harder on skills, retention and career progression.

02

Greece: the stronger recent recovery.

A 6.12-point fall in unemployment is real progress. Its 41.6% summer concentration makes extending the tourism season the next priority.

03

Our lesson: make growth last longer.

A fuller annual calendar gives local businesses more reasons to keep staff. We favour linking tourism investment with training and lasting employment.

More of the year, more opportunity.

If July–August nights stay at their 2025 levels, these growth ambitions would lower the summer share from about 26% to about 23% in Portugal, and from 41.6% to 35.4% in Greece. We would use that extra demand to support longer operating seasons and more continuous work.

HOW WE WOULD GET THERE

Put the extra demand to work.

The growth ambitions are deliberately higher for Greece because its seasonal imbalance is larger. Portugal's employment ambition requires faster improvement than its 2021–2025 pace; Greece's would extend its recovery at a slower pace.

Back to our hotel worker: our hope is a longer working season and a clearer career path. Portugal shows the stronger starting point; Greece shows how much progress is possible. By 2030, we want growth to mean more opportunity throughout the year.
